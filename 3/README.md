# Experiment 3: Earth's Magnetic Field, Vacuum Permeability, and Helmholtz Coils

This directory contains the experimental data, analysis scripts, generated plots, and LaTeX report for **Experiment 3: Measurement of Earth's Magnetic Field, Vacuum Permeability ($\mu_0$), and Field Distribution of Helmholtz Coils** in Physics Laboratory II at Sharif University of Technology.

---

## Overview

The primary objectives of this experiment are:
1. **Axial Field Mapping:** Investigate the spatial distribution of the axial magnetic field $B(D)$ generated by a pair of circular coaxial coils, comparing the standard Helmholtz geometry ($a = R$) against a reduced coil spacing ($a = R/2$).
2. **Vacuum Permeability ($\mu_0$):** Measure the magnetic field $B_H$ at the center of the Helmholtz coil pair as a function of excitation current $I$, determining the coil constant $K$ and extracting the magnetic permeability of free space $\mu_0$ via Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression.
3. **Earth's Horizontal Magnetic Field ($B_E^h$):** Configure a tangent galvanometer at the center of the coil system with its axis aligned perpendicular to the magnetic meridian, and extract the horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field through linear regression of $I \cdot K$ against $\tan\alpha$.
4. **Earth's Vertical Component ($B_E^v$) and Total Field ($|B_E|$):** Measure the magnetic dip (inclination) angle $\nu$ to calculate the vertical component and total magnitude of Earth's geomagnetic field with full error propagation.

## Theoretical Background

### 1. Magnetic Field of a Circular Current Loop and Helmholtz Coils

From the Biot–Savart law, the axial magnetic field produced by a single circular turn of radius $R$ carrying current $I$ at distance $x$ from its center along the axis of symmetry is:

```
B_single(x) = (mu_0 * I * R^2) / (2 * (R^2 + x^2)^(3/2))
```

For two coaxial coils with $n$ turns each, separated by an axial distance $a$, with currents flowing in the same direction, the total axial field as a function of displacement $D$ from the midpoint ($D = 0$) is obtained by superposition:

```
B(D) = (mu_0 * n * I * R^2 / 2) * [ 1 / (R^2 + (D - a/2)^2)^(3/2) + 1 / (R^2 + (D + a/2)^2)^(3/2) ]
```

#### The Helmholtz Condition ($a = R$)
By symmetry, all odd-order derivatives of $B(D)$ vanish at the midpoint $D = 0$:

```
dB/dD |_{D=0} = 0,    d^3B/dD^3 |_{D=0} = 0
```

Setting the second derivative to zero at the origin eliminates the leading quadratic curvature term:

```
d^2B/dD^2 |_{D=0} = 0  ==>  a = R
```

Under this condition, the Taylor series expansion around $D = 0$ yields:

```
B(D) = B(0) + (1/24) * (d^4B/dD^4)|_{0} * D^4 + O(D^6)
```

This guarantees an exceptionally uniform magnetic field throughout the central inter-coil volume. At $D = 0$, the central magnetic field is:

```
B(0) = (4/5)^(3/2) * (mu_0 * n * I / R) = (8 / (5 * sqrt(5))) * (mu_0 * n / R) * I = K * I
```

where the geometric coil constant $K$ is defined as:

```
K = (8 / (5 * sqrt(5))) * (mu_0 * n / R)
```

Solving for the permeability of free space $\mu_0$:

```
mu_0 = (5 * sqrt(5) / 8) * (K * R / n)
```

### 2. Tangent Galvanometer Principle (Earth's Horizontal Field $B_E^h$)

A magnetic compass needle placed at the center of the coil pair aligns itself along the local magnetic meridian (the direction of Earth's horizontal magnetic field $\mathbf{B}_E^h$). When the axis of the Helmholtz coils is oriented strictly perpendicular to $\mathbf{B}_E^h$, the coil field $\mathbf{B}_H = K I \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{axis}}$ and Earth's field $\mathbf{B}_E^h$ form two mutually orthogonal vectors in the horizontal plane:

```
B_net = B_E^h + B_H,    where B_H _|_ B_E^h
```

The compass needle deflects by an angle $\alpha$ relative to the magnetic meridian such that:

```
tan(alpha) = B_H / B_E^h = (K * I) / B_E^h
```

Rearranging into linear form:

```
I * K = B_E^h * tan(alpha)
```

Plotting $y = I \cdot K$ versus $x = \tan\alpha$ yields a straight line through the origin whose slope directly represents $B_E^h$.

### 3. Vertical Component and Total Geomagnetic Field

By rotating the magnetometer into a vertical plane aligned with the magnetic meridian, the dip needle measures the inclination angle $\nu$ relative to the horizontal:

```
B_E^v = B_E^h * tan(nu)
|B_E| = sqrt((B_E^h)^2 + (B_E^v)^2)
```

### 4. Uncertainty Propagation

Uncertainties are evaluated using first-order partial derivative quadrature:

```
delta_mu0 = mu_0 * sqrt( (delta_K / K)^2 + (delta_R / R)^2 )

delta_B_Ev = sqrt( (tan(nu) * delta_B_Eh)^2 + (B_E^h * sec^2(nu) * delta_nu_rad)^2 )

delta_|B_E| = (1 / |B_E|) * sqrt( (B_E^h * delta_B_Eh)^2 + (B_E^v * delta_B_Ev)^2 )
```

## Experimental Setup

- **Helmholtz Coils:** Two coaxial circular coils with $n = 154$ turns per coil, nominal radius $R = 20.0 \pm 0.2\text{ cm}$ ($0.200\text{ m}$).
- **DC Power Supply:** Regulated current source with fine adjustment controls.
- **Multimeters:** Digital DC ammeter (uncertainty $\pm 0.01\text{ A}$) and precision milliammeter ($\pm 1\text{ mA}$).
- **Teslameter:** Hall-effect axial probe mounted on a graduated non-magnetic optical bench (resolution $\pm 0.01\text{ mT}$, spatial resolution $\pm 0.2\text{ cm}$).
- **Tangent Compass / Inclinometer:** Rotatable magnetic needle on an angular dial calibrated in degrees ($\pm 1^\circ$).

---

## Key Analyses

- **Axial Magnetic Field Profile ($B_H$ vs. $D$):**
  - Experimental points measured from $D = 0$ to $D = 50\text{ cm}$ at $I = \text{const}$.
  - Comparison with theoretical models for both the Helmholtz spacing ($a = R$) and the reduced spacing ($a = R/2$).
  - Analysis of field uniformity: while the Helmholtz configuration exhibits a flat central plateau ($d^2B/dD^2 = 0$), the reduced configuration ($a = R/2$) produces a sharp central maximum with steep radial/axial gradients.
- **Vacuum Permeability ($\mu_0$) Regression:**
  - OLS fit of $B_H$ vs. $I$ over 9 points ($0.20\text{ A}$ to $1.77\text{ A}$).
  - Extraction of experimental $K = (8.512 \pm 0.089) \times 10^{-4}\text{ T/A}$ ($R^2 = 0.9992$).
  - Deduction of $\mu_0 = (1.545 \pm 0.022) \times 10^{-6}\text{ H/m}$.
- **Tangent Galvanometer Linear Regression:**
  - 10 deflection angles $\alpha$ from $14^\circ$ to $74^\circ$ for currents $10\text{ mA}$ to $100\text{ mA}$.
  - OLS fit of $I \cdot K$ vs. $\tan\alpha$ yields $B_E^h = (24.77 \pm 0.94)\,\mu\text{T}$ ($R^2 = 0.9885$).
- **Dip Angle and Total Field Vector:**
  - Inclination angle $\nu = 55.0^\circ \pm 1.0^\circ$.
  - Vertical component $B_E^v = (35.38 \pm 1.88)\,\mu\text{T}$.
  - Total magnitude $|B_E| = (43.19 \pm 1.63)\,\mu\text{T}$.

## Notes

1. **Systematic Offset in $\mu_0$:** The experimental value of $\mu_0$ exceeds the theoretical constant $4\pi\times 10^{-7}\text{ H/m}$ by $\approx 23\%$. This discrepancy is typical for instructional laboratory setups and arises from:
   - Non-zero cross-sectional winding dimensions (the finite pack thickness and width of the multi-layer winding deviates from an ideal thin filamentary current ring).
   - Teslameter probe calibration uncertainty at low fields (near $1\text{ mT}$).
   - External magnetic materials (screws, optical rail, bench supports) slightly perturbing the magnetic permeability in the vicinity.
2. **Earth's Field Concordance:** The extracted horizontal component $B_E^h \approx 24.8\,\mu\text{T}$ and dip angle $\nu \approx 55^\circ$ align closely with the World Magnetic Model (WMM) and geomagnetic survey data for Tehran (latitude $\approx 35.7^\circ\text{N}$, expected $B_E^h \approx 25\text{--}26\,\mu\text{T}$, inclination $\approx 54^\circ\text{--}56^\circ$).
3. **Orthogonality Requirement:** Accurate measurement of $B_E^h$ with the tangent galvanometer strictly demands that the coil axis be oriented perpendicular to the magnetic north-south axis. Any angular deviation introduces systematic errors in $\tan\alpha$ by projecting an unwanted component of $B_H$ along $B_E^h$.
